## Chapter walkthrough

### Canal Drowning Hazard
- Public urinals in the Netherlands address the danger of drunk tourists, particularly British males, falling into canals and drowning while urinating.
- "Piss-related drownings" are a significant cause of death, often identified by victims' unzipped flies.
- Alcohol consumption lowers blood pressure, and emptying the bladder accelerates this process, leading to light-headedness and stumbling.
- Canals are inherently difficult to climb out of, further increasing the risk for inebriated individuals.
- Impaired motor function due to intoxication exacerbates the danger of falling into the water.

### The Plaskrul Solution
- The "Plaskrul" (piss curl) was the first major solution, constructed from affordable, long-lasting cast iron bent into a golden ratio curve.
- Its design features an open bottom to indicate occupancy without revealing the user, ensuring a degree of privacy.
- The urinal's bladder-shaped legs were intended to provide intuitive understanding of its purpose.
- A dark green color was chosen for the Plaskrul to help it blend seamlessly into the urban cityscape.
- Older models incorporate Saint Andrew's crosses, which serve as city advertising, ventilation, and a viewpoint for the user.
- The design also includes Dutch tiling and bricks, likely sourced from the Rhine region, contributing to its local aesthetic.
- Plaskruls are strategically positioned near water bodies and in tourist-heavy areas like the Red Light District.

### Evolution to Uri-lift
- Amsterdam's government is removing Plaskruls due to several drawbacks, including their lack of space efficiency.
- Plaskruls are not connected to a municipal water system, necessitating manual cleaning, which is labor-intensive.
- Attempts to develop handicap-accessible and female-friendly versions of the Plaskrul were unsuccessful.
- The "Uri-lift" is introduced as the successor, a retractable urinal that emerges from the ground specifically at night.
- This modern solution is integrated into the city's water management system, allowing for self-rinsing capabilities.

### Uri-lift Engineering
- The Uri-lift is constructed from corrosion-resistant brushed stainless steel, ensuring durability and longevity.
- Its single-pillar design is capable of serving three users simultaneously, optimizing space utilization.
- The structure is precisely cut to provide adequate privacy for users.
- Unlike the Plaskrul, some Uri-lift models are designed to be unisex and handicap-accessible, addressing previous limitations.
- The video draws a comparison between the Uri-lift's engineering and that of prominent Dutch companies like ASML, highlighting its advanced design.
- The Uri-lift system is described as AI-powered, indicating advanced operational capabilities.
