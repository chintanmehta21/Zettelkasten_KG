## Counterarguments
- Psychedelics can lead to the adoption of specific, often dogmatic or unconventional beliefs (e.g., simulation theory, spiritualism, crystal magic) rather than fostering general skepticism.
- Skepticism induced by psychedelics, without accompanying critical thinking skills, can lead individuals to embrace conspiracy theories or pseudo-science.
- Some users report that psychedelic experiences reinforce their existing worldviews, such as physicalism, or are purely recreational with no significant impact on their approach to certainty or skepticism.
- The claim that psychedelics disrupt the certainty of truth might be a subjective experience that cannot be generalized, and empirical studies sometimes show increased optimism, not just skepticism.
- Other experiences, such as studying astronomy or cosmology, can disrupt the certainty of truth more profoundly than psychedelics.
- The article's evidence, relying on a single individual's journal, may be insufficient to support broad claims about psychedelic experiences universally exposing cognitive limits or avoiding dogmatic insights.

## Unresolved Questions
- How can subjective experience, particularly under the influence of psychedelics, be reconciled with a purely physicalist understanding of consciousness?
- What is the precise philosophical definition of 'truth' being disrupted by psychedelics, and how does it relate to objective reality versus subjective perception?
- What proportion of psychedelic users genuinely develop profound skepticism versus those who adopt new, potentially dogmatic or unconventional beliefs?
- How can critical thinking skills be effectively integrated or taught alongside the radical skepticism that psychedelics might induce, to prevent misdirection towards misinformation?
