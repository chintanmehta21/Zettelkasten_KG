## Research Context and Methodology
- Prior small-sample-size research on psychedelics and brain connectivity yielded inconsistent and contradictory findings.
- Girn and colleagues performed a mega-analysis of 11 different magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) datasets.
- The study was published in Nature Medicine.
- Psychedelics are compounds that primarily target serotonin receptors.
- Researchers re-analyzed raw resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) data from multiple laboratories.
- A single analysis pipeline was used to reduce inconsistencies across datasets.
- Most included datasets were from randomized controlled trials with a placebo condition.
- The analysis aimed to test a previous finding that psychedelics decrease connectivity within networks and increase it between networks.

## Key Findings
- Compared to placebo, psychedelics robustly increased functional connectivity between networks.
- This increase was especially noted in networks for attention, self-reflection, sensory processing, and executive function.
- The previously hypothesized decrease in connectivity within networks was much less robust.
- An exception was a single study on ayahuasca with 9 participants and no placebo, which showed an overall decrease in connectivity.

## Implications
- These findings provide more support for some theories of psychedelic action (e.g., the subcortical connectivity account) over others (e.g., the 'network disintegration' account).
- The results can help inform the development of therapies for neuropsychiatric disorders.
- The findings can contribute to personalized medicine approaches.
- The research can inform regulations related to psychedelics.
