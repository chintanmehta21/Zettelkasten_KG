## Chapter walkthrough

### Q-Day and Quantum Computing Fundamentals
- Major tech companies like Google, Microsoft, and IBM are preparing for "Q-day," the moment quantum computers can break all existing encryption.
- Unlike classical computers using bits (0 or 1), quantum computers use qubits, which can exist in a superposition of both states simultaneously.
- This superposition allows quantum computers to solve certain problems exponentially faster than classical machines.
- Modern encryption, such as RSA-2048, relies on the classical difficulty of factoring large numbers into prime factors.
- A classical supercomputer would take millions of years to break RSA-2048, but a quantum computer could do it in hours.

### Quantum Progress and Cryptographic Vulnerability
- Peter Shor's 1994 algorithm demonstrated how a sufficiently powerful quantum computer could efficiently break current encryption.
- Breaking real-world encryption requires millions of stable qubits, a goal that is becoming increasingly closer.
- In December 2024, Google's 105-qubit "Willow" chip showed improved error rates with scale, solving a problem in 5 minutes that would take a classical supercomputer 10 septillion years.
- Microsoft announced its "Majorana 1" chip in February 2025, featuring more stable topological qubits.
- IBM has outlined a roadmap aiming for fault-tolerant quantum systems by the end of the current decade.
- China is also making significant investments, committing $15 billion to quantum research.

### Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration
- The Global Risk Institute estimates Q-day will arrive within 10-15 years, prompting urgent action.
- The US NIST is planning for a comprehensive migration to post-quantum cryptography (PQC) before 2035.
- On Q-day, critical digital infrastructure like secure banking, private messaging, and signed software updates will become compromised.
- A significant threat is the "harvest now, decrypt later" strategy, where adversaries collect encrypted data today to decrypt it with future quantum computers.
- Migrating the entire internet to PQC is an immense and complex undertaking, expected to be uneven across different entities.
- Wealthy nations and large financial institutions are likely to upgrade first, potentially fragmenting the global internet into tiers and eroding trust in digital transactions.

### Beyond Encryption: Quantum's Impact
- Beyond breaking encryption, quantum computers can solve problems currently impossible for classical machines, which are reaching physical limits.
- Quantum computers can also create novel materials, such as simulating a nitrogen-fixing enzyme.
- They offer optimization capabilities for complex systems in finance, with Goldman Sachs and JP Morgan exploring their use.
- Access to this advanced technology will be highly restricted to a few dozen institutions due to its high cost and complexity.
- This limited access will create a significant power imbalance, contrasting with the democratized nature of the digital age and defining the post-digital era by who controls these powerful machines.

## Closing remarks
- Recap: Quantum computing represents a fundamental shift in technological capability, threatening current digital security and opening new frontiers for scientific and industrial innovation. The transition to a quantum-resistant world will be complex and will redefine global power structures based on access to this advanced computational power.
