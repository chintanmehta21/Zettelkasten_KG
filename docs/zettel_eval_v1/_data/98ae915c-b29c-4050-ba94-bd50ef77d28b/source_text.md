## OP Intent
- To explore if the perceptual and experiential changes induced by psychedelics indicate that human perception of reality is incomplete or that aspects of a broader reality are typically missed.

## Reply Clusters
- Human perception is inherently limited and incomplete: Biological and evolutionary constraints mean our senses only capture a fraction of available information, filtering out much for survival. Other species perceive realities inaccessible to humans.
- Psychedelics alter brain function, leading to novel but not necessarily 'truer' perceptions: Psychedelics create temporary new neural pathways, dissolve mental habits, and increase cross-hemispheric communication, leading to altered processing of sensory data. This is often seen as a change within the 'neurological universe' of the brain rather than direct access to an external 'broader reality.'
- Skepticism regarding claims of 'broader reality' or 'expanded consciousness' via psychedelics: Altered states are simply different experiences, potentially due to brain malfunction or mis-firings, rather than revealing a more fundamental truth. Scientific understanding of brain chemistry offers a more grounded explanation than metaphysical speculation.
- Philosophical and metaphysical interpretations of reality and consciousness: Reality might transcend mathematical or physical reduction, existing as something more fundamental. Consciousness could be expressed through the brain rather than solely produced by it, or it could be a property of matter. Quantum phenomena already suggest a reality far stranger than everyday perception.

### Human perception is inherently limited and incomplete
- Bats' echolocation, birds' quantum navigation, marine electroreception
- Inability to perceive infrared/ultraviolet light
- The brain's role in filtering sensory input for survival
- Perceiving an infinitesimally small portion of the universe or EM spectrum

### Psychedelics alter brain function, leading to novel but not necessarily 'truer' perceptions
- Psilocybin creating new brain pathways and processing data differently
- The brain as a 'squishy computer' with altered circuits
- Nervous system activity 'overflowing' boundaries of established structures
- Delivering 'raw form of sensory input' by disrupting conceptual schemes

### Skepticism regarding claims of 'broader reality' or 'expanded consciousness' via psychedelics
- Comparing psychedelic experiences to being high on MDMA or whiskey, not revealing 'true love of the universe'
- Psychedelic drugs interfering with synaptic functions, causing 'malfunction' and models that do not work
- The notion of 'expanding consciousness' being problematic; altered consciousness is just another variety of experience
- The brain 'malfunctioning' by making connections between parts typically not in communication

### Philosophical and metaphysical interpretations of reality and consciousness
- Reality existing beyond universal physical laws or mathematical reduction
- Consciousness as a subjective flux of qualia
- The brain as a 'tool' that allows greater expression of consciousness, rather than its source
- Quantum entanglement, wave function collapse, and Einstein's GR predicting parallel universes

## Counterarguments
- Psychedelics merely induce a 'high' or a state of brain 'malfunction' rather than revealing deeper truths about reality.
- The notion of 'expanding consciousness' is problematic, as altered states are simply different varieties of experience, not necessarily 'more real' or 'broader.'
- The brain is not accurately described as a computer, and analogies implying it processes 'data' or 'information' in a digital sense are flawed.
- The simplest explanation for altered consciousness is electrochemical activity in the brain, without needing to appeal to unsupported metaphysical beliefs.
- Psychedelics might affect senses, but not necessarily consciousness itself.

## Unresolved Questions
- Are all possible realities fixed on universal physical laws, or do some exist beyond mathematical reduction or definition?
- What would a 'complete perception of reality' entail, and is it even possible for a conscious entity?
- Can we perceive quantum systems without collapsing their wave functions?
- What is the smallest viable system capable of self-awareness, and how can consciousness be defined at a fundamental level?
- How can boundaries of perception be drawn, and what does 'expanding' them truly mean in terms of experience?
