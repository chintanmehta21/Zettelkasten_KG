## Scope and Origin of the Leak
- A leak of 11.5 million records from the Panamanian law firm Mossack Fonseca, covering 1977 through 2015.
- Exposed the offshore holdings of 12 current and former world leaders and 128 other politicians and public officials.
- Investigation conducted over a year by the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ), German newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung, and over 100 other news organizations, involving more than 370 journalists from 76 countries.
- Contains information on 214,488 offshore entities connected to people in over 200 countries, operating in 21 offshore jurisdictions.

## Prominent Individuals and Entities Implicated
- Associates of Russian President Vladimir Putin moved as much as $2 billion through banks and shadow companies, using a network linked to Bank Rossiya; transactions included a $200 million loan passed through four companies and two banks in 24 hours, with interest rights landing in a company controlled by Putin's friend, Sergey Roldugin.
- The prime minister of Iceland (Sigmundur David Gunnlaugsson), the king of Saudi Arabia, and the children of the president of Azerbaijan and the prime minister of Pakistan.
- China's Xi Jinping (via his brother-in-law), Ukrainian President Petro Poroshenko, and Argentina's President Mauricio Macri.
- The late father of British Prime Minister David Cameron, Ian Cameron, used the firm to shield his investment fund from UK taxes.
- Soccer player Lionel Messi and his father were also owners of a Panama company.

## Mossack Fonseca's Business Practices and Clientele
- Worked with over 14,000 middlemen, including over 500 banks; UBS set up over 1,100 offshore companies and HSBC over 2,300.
- Serviced at least 33 people and companies blacklisted by the U.S. government for ties to Mexican drug lords, Hezbollah, North Korea, and Iran.
- One such company allegedly supplied fuel for Syrian government aircraft used to bomb citizens.
- Clients included 29 billionaires from Forbes' list, Jackie Chan (with no evidence of improper use).
- Involved in scandals like the 1983 Brink's-Mat gold heist, where the firm helped protect a company tied to a money launderer.
- In the FIFA scandal, the law firm of ethics committee member Juan Pedro Damiani had business relationships with three indicted men.

## Controversial Practices of Mossack Fonseca
- Offered to backdate documents for a fee ($8.75 per month in 2007).
- Removed paper records and wiped electronic data from its Las Vegas branch to evade a U.S. court order.
- Destroyed documents during Brazil's "Operation Car Wash" investigation.
- In a case involving convicted money launderer Malchus Irvin Boncamper, who was a nominee director for 30 companies, the firm backdated records to replace him.
- Created offshore companies for individuals involved in the Fidentia fraud in South Africa, which looted a $60 million death benefits fund for 46,000 mineworkers' widows and orphans.
- A 2015 internal audit revealed the firm knew the identities of the real owners of just 204 of 14,086 companies it incorporated in the Seychelles.

## Mossack Fonseca's Defense
- Co-founder Ramón Fonseca compared the firm to a "car factory" not liable for how its products are used.
- The firm stated it does not promote illegal acts and that backdating is an accepted industry practice.
