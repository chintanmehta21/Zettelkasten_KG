## Overview and History of Silk Road 1
- SR1 was an online drug marketplace rooted in the cypherpunk movement's economic and cryptographic principles.
- Operated as a Tor hidden service and used Bitcoin exclusively.
- Gained public attention from US Senators Schumer and Manchin after a 2011 Gawker article.
- Flourished until its bust on 2013-10-02.

## Market Size and Competitors
- SR1 size estimates varied significantly across different analyses.
- Monitoring reviews suggested ~100 daily transactions at ~$150 each over two years.
- Christin 2013's 8-month analysis (2011–2012) calculated a $1.2m monthly turnover (~$15m annually) and $92k monthly commissions by July 2012.
- A DHS estimate suggested Mt. Gox moved $60m/month to darknet markets in May 2013.
- Blockchain analysis found one possible SR1 address with ₿450,825 ($2.88m) on 2012-06-23, implying >$28m total turnover at a 5-10% commission.
- Competitors included BlackMarket Reloaded (BMR), which grew to ~$700k monthly turnover by Feb 2013 before a temporary shutdown.
- Atlantis launched in March 2013, turned over >$500k by June, but shut down in Sept 2013 citing an FBI leak about Tor vulnerabilities.
- Sheep Marketplace started in early 2013 and exit-scammed with >₿39,644 in Nov 2013.
- Deepbay also reportedly stole user funds.

## Ideological and Technical Foundations
- The cypherpunk paradigm provided the ideological foundation, summarized as replacing centralized coercive systems with decentralized voluntary ones enforced by math and economics.
- Bitcoin solved the double-spend problem decentrally through proof-of-work, making it a suitable currency for SR1.
- SR1's design, resembling Timothy C. May's 1994 "Cyphernomicon," used an anonymizing network, pseudonyms, public-key cryptography, seller bonds, public ratings, and crucial escrow services to align incentives.

## Operational Aspects and Security
- The failure to use escrow, known as "early finalization" (FE), was a primary mechanism for scams, exemplified by the Tony76 scam in April 2012 which netted over $50,000.
- FBI lab tests of over 70 purchases found high purity in 54 of 56 tested samples.
- A DHS agent testified that of >50 orders, all but 1-2 delivered the advertised drug.
- Leaked law enforcement reports indicated SR1 had strong operational security, with PGP being a major obstacle.
- The author identified DDoS attacks and mass creation of fake accounts as primary vulnerabilities.
- A single individual's DDoS had previously taken the site down for weeks.

## User Experience and Case Studies
- The author detailed personal experiences using Tor and Bitcoin mixers to purchase selegiline, Adderall, and armodafinil.
- The process involved vetting sellers, encrypting addresses with PGP, and receiving discreetly packaged goods.
- A detailed case study on purchasing LSD demonstrated a systematic approach to evaluating sellers using a table compiled on 2012-09-03.
- The LSD case study applied statistical analysis (binomial proportion confidence intervals) to feedback scores to create a risk-weighted value metric (μg/₿), and compared prices to a Dutch bulk seller.

## Future Speculations and Recommendations
- The article speculated on future developments like information markets and assassination markets.
- Skepticism was expressed about assassination markets due to a trust-bootstrapping problem.
- A post-mortem section offered recommendations for future darknet market operators, including aggressive data deletion, mandatory PGP, pre-committed shutdown plans, and a nested VM server architecture.
