### Core argument

This application focuses on achieving deeper understanding and overcoming obstacles in complex, real-world challenges, rather than general creativity or learning new subjects.

## Chapter walkthrough

### Introduction and Context
- The speaker initiates the discussion by referencing an online comment that highlights the cultural and ritualistic context (set and setting) as crucial for psychedelic effects, beyond the substance itself.
- He notes the parallel emergence of personal computers, Silicon Valley, Burning Man, and increased psychedelic use during a specific historical period.
- The primary focus of his talk is on the under-researched area of utilizing psychedelics to enhance problem-solving abilities.
- This research aims to help experts achieve a deeper understanding of complex, real-world problems rather than teaching new subjects.
- The speaker contrasts this specific application with general creativity research, where known effective methods are often underutilized across various sectors.

### Research Framework and Problem Validation
- The speaker's research group established specific guidelines concerning set, setting, substance, and dosage to facilitate scientists working on real-world problems.
- The study targeted problems that were measurable, provable, buildable, or patentable, ensuring concrete outcomes.
- A solution was validated if the problem was solved or closer to resolution, an obstacle was overcome, and the psychedelic session was identified as the direct cause.
- The speaker dismisses the concept of 'unnatural' molecules, emphasizing the chemical nature of substances.
- This application of psychedelics for problem-solving was novel in the late 1960s, differing from traditional indigenous uses for shaman-led diagnosis and divination.

### Psychedelic Effects on Problem-Solving
- Potential hindrances from psychedelic use include diminished logic, reduced concentration, uncontrolled imagery, anxiety, constricted communication, and a tendency to view real-world tasks as trivial.
- Conversely, benefits encompass access to unconscious data, free association, and spontaneous play with hypotheses.
- Participants also experienced heightened visual imagery, relaxation, openness, and heightened sensory input.
- Other advantages included empathy with objects, an enhanced sense of 'rightness' to discern false solutions, and lessened inhibitions.
- A heightened motivation to solve the problem was also observed as a significant benefit.

### Participant and Problem Selection
- The research primarily used synthetic psychedelics like LSD and mescaline, as their effects are more influenced by set and setting compared to substances like ayahuasca.
- Participant selection criteria required individuals to be obsessed with a problem and possess the necessary technical knowledge, often being paid to solve it.
- Participants must have worked on their problem for months without success, ideally feeling frustrated or angry about their failure.
- This high level of motivation was considered comparable to that of a person with a serious disease seeking a cure.
- A sample of the 44 problems attempted included photoconductivity decay, vibratory microtome design, solar probe experiments, and architectural designs.

### Study Outcomes and Lasting Effects
- Solutions were found for all but four of the 44 problems attempted during the study.
- One architect reported a massive increase in concentration, creating three client-accepted designs in three hours, feeling he became a 'better' version of himself.
- This architect also solved a problem in 20 minutes that would typically take weeks and later created complex working drawings from memory.
- Other participants described breaking 'airtight deadlocks' and experiencing a feverish flow of ideas, with an engineer detailing a complex visualization for a thermal imaging device.
- The study used 100 micrograms of LSD or 200 milligrams of mescaline interchangeably, finding no difference in effectiveness.
- After the session, participants reported 1 to 8 weeks of enhanced creativity, with one participant becoming a serious artist and retaining the benefit without further psychedelic use.

### Protocol and Future Vision
- The speaker advises against using psychedelics to learn new subjects, though he shares an anecdote of a student acing an exam by visualizing lecture slides.
- The preferred protocol involves a sober guide providing minimal intervention, with the participant lying down with headphones and music.
- The speaker concludes by quoting Julie Holland, stating, 'It is unethical not to be doing psychedelic research.'.
- He envisions a future culture that embraces freedom of science and personal exploration.
- This future views psychedelics as another tool for productivity, akin to how companies provide coffee to employees.
