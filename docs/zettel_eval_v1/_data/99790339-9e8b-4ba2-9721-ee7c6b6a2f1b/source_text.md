- The original poster seeks philosophical perspectives and personal experiences regarding hallucinogenic drugs, particularly how they challenge worldviews, demonstrate the fragility of the mind, and raise fundamental questions about reality, consciousness, and language. They are interested in long, detailed posts from those who have experimented or abstained.

## Reply Clusters
- Psychedelics as tools for philosophical insight and altered perception: Users report that these substances enable them to approach problems from different angles, offer introspective experiences, and reveal the brain's capacity for novel thought patterns. They can lead to questioning the nature of reality, language's role in structuring experience, and the concept of ego.
- Specific drug experiences and recommendations: Different substances are described as offering distinct types of experiences, with some being highlighted for their profound philosophical impact (e.g., DMT, psilocybin) and others deemed less useful (e.g., dissociatives, opiates). This suggests that the "meaning" derived is not inherent in the drug but constructed by the experiencer.
- Re-evaluating concepts of Ego and Reality: Users describe experiences like "ego death" or perceiving all states of consciousness as a "spectrum of hallucination," leading to questions about the objective baseline of reality and the function of the ego. Some argue ego is a "narrative device" to be embraced, not abolished.

### Psychedelics as tools for philosophical insight and altered perception
- piecing together the disparate fragments of my shattered world-view after each bout I have with hallucinogens I have found to be a very rewarding experience.
- some of my best undergraduate philosophy papers were the result. I don't necessarily believe that cannabis is entirely responsible, but I do believe that it (in the right amounts) enabled me to approach problems from a different angle (perhaps more intuitively?).
- Part of my philosophical reaction to psychedelic experiences has been a shift in the sorts of questions that I ask myself. Instead of testing my ability to distinguish between reality and unreality, I am more interested in the question, What is reality at all? Why is there stuff here? Why is there first-personal experience?
- I believe psychedelics are tools that give you an a ability to temporarily or permanently control the perception and the assembly of reality. Including the ability to achieve heightened states of awareness that are often inaccessible to an individual raised in language based society.
- LSD specifically gave me a chance to look at me (and the world around me) from outside of my own constraints and restraints. It definitely made me realize the power of language, and what a dramatic shift in brain development its use must have triggered.
- Psychedelics have given me deep philosophical and introspective experiences where I learned that my brain was capable of thinking in ways I had never before thought possible.

### Specific drug experiences and recommendations
- If you believe in God, you really should try psilocybin mushrooms. It isn't as "unreal" as you think.
- You should try low doses of psilocybin mushrooms and work your way up as you become more comfortable; you'll definitely appreciate it. When I say low, I mean .25g, .5g, etc;
- Philosophically speaking, I don't think you can get ANYTHING out of any drug ( that I've tried ), that you can't also get out of smoking DMT. Acid and mushrooms can only approach what you get from Dimitri.
- Tryptamines and phenethylamines are generally an awesome way to see things from another perspective... Other categories like dissociatives and opiates are mostly useless in a philosophical context.
- My experiences of DMT have been similar. During my first "full blown" trip I saw a "geometric humanoid elf".
- The result of taking a hit of 15x extract of salvia while tripping on acid was divine. Instead of the usual mind-fuck that characterizes salvia experiences, the time-dilated loop effect allowed me stop and critically analyze the trip and its implications

### Cautionary notes, risks, and limitations
- Cautionary note about Terrence McKenna: interesting fellow, but you have to take everything he says with a huge grain of salt because he eventually turned into a washed out hippie guru way too invested in his own bullshit. ...if that is all you ever think about - you *will* very likely begin to lose your grip on reality.
- Hasjisj are a known psychosis trigger. Shrooms are not.
- My personal moral boundary lies at my ability to determine what is reality and what is not. I know from smoking weed that I am too neurotic to ever try anything stronger.
- I only know that I can't get my sober self to take my drug influenced self seriously.
- I immediately doubt claims that were revealed to people by drugs nor do I find any arguments from the major drugist philosophists (they're not philosophers, let's be serious) the least bit convincing. I think that people who claim to find something profound are merely more evidence for a self-confirming bias.
- I took acid *once*. It was enough for me. Some things, for me anyway, are better left unknown.

### The subjective and worldview-dependent nature of drug experiences
- So much of it depends on your worldview. I believe in God and I find it fascinating that a plant exists which acts so uniquely in the human brain so as to alter experience.
- You can't pin down exactly what these experiences are apart from the experiencer. So any discussion about what hallucinogens do must also consider the individual who is taking the drug and experiencing its effects. For example, a person who believes in voodoo will have a very different description from a person raised in Western modern culture.
- Well really, thoughtful philosophic types will get a profounder meaning out of anything.

### Re-evaluating concepts of Ego and Reality
- Some would say that Ego death , or a state of being without an Ego, is a pure, objective state, where you are able to truly exist. Those people would say that we are blinded by our innate subjective ego and preconceptions.
- I've experienced the "death of the ego", but during one of my visions it was made perfectly clear that ego is in fact a GOOD thing. This being because ego is a great narrative device ( if this makes any sense ).
- To me, all states of consciousness seem to exist within a multidimensional spectrum of hallucination.
- Psychedelic experiences are just as "real" as our "normal" experiences. What makes them categorically distinct from "normal" experiences? I can't imagine how one could find a solid grounding from determining an absolute baseline of "reality" in terms of human brain chemistry.

## Counterarguments
- The idea that drug-induced insights are merely self-confirming biases, not genuine philosophical revelations.
- The assertion that perception is outside the head, directly accessing the world, rather than an internal representation that can be "controlled" or "reassembled" by drugs.
- Skepticism about the philosophical utility of certain drug categories (e.g., dissociatives, opiates) or the profoundness of drug experiences in general for those "too firmly on the ground."
- The argument that the "sober self" cannot take the "drugged self" seriously, implying a lack of lasting philosophical value.
- The view that "messing with your brain" is an insufficient explanation for profound psychedelic experiences, implying something more than mere neurochemical alteration.
- A strong rejection of radical reductionism, arguing that denying first-person experience or reducing thoughts to "atoms" is naive and misses the essence of philosophical inquiry into human experience.

## Unresolved Questions
- What constitutes "reality" itself, beyond the distinction between "reality" and "unreality" in altered states?
- What is consciousness or first-personal experience, and is it categorically similar to physical stuff?
- To what extent does language structure our experience, and can psychedelics offer access to states of awareness inaccessible in a language-based society?
- Is there an objective baseline for "reality" in terms of human brain chemistry, or are all states of consciousness part of a spectrum of hallucination?
- How can one reconcile the profound subjective experiences of psychedelics with a naturalist or reductionist view of the brain?
- What is the true nature and function of the ego, and should it be abolished or embraced?
