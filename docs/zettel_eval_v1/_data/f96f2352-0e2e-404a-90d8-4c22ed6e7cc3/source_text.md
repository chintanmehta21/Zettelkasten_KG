## Overview
- This analysis examines an FT piece by Gita Gopinath, her cautionary stance on heterodox economic policies.

### Publication
- An analysis of an FT piece by Gita Gopinath.

### Stance
- The piece weighs mixed views.

### Core argument
- In an era of "polycrisis" and constrained fiscal space, heterodox economic measures, often viewed negatively, may become necessary alternatives to traditional playbooks, challenging market-centric objections.

## Section walkthrough

### Gopinath's Warning and Traditional Playbook
- Gita Gopinath warns that governments with limited fiscal space might resort to "heterodox measures" if a new crisis playbook is not established.
- These measures include broad-based price controls, financial repression, nationalizations, and pressuring central banks to absorb fiscal risk.
- Her proposed playbook emphasizes targeted support for vulnerable populations and bailouts exclusively for viable, liquidity-constrained firms posing systemic risks.
- Gopinath also advocates for coordinated fiscal-monetary policy to manage crises effectively.
- She cautions that adopting heterodox measures would trigger synchronized sell-offs in stocks and bonds, as markets would perceive the absence of their expected backstop.

### The Author's Challenge and "Polycrisis" Context
- Prompted by Daniela Gabor's tweet, the author questions whether these "Four Horsemen of Heterodoxy" should be rejected or considered.
- The article posits a "polycrisis" or "permanent crisis" scenario, citing Meadway and Weber et al., characterized by repeated shocks like COVID and the Ukraine conflict.
- This environment, coupled with declining US hegemony, pushes centrist politicians towards large-scale interventions to counter right-wing populism.
- However, these politicians, often lacking a progressive agenda (e.g., UK's Keir Starmer), tend to revert to fiscal rules when economic difficulties arise.
- This pattern leads to increasing debt and eventual fiscal constraints, making heterodox options the primary alternative to further expanding public liabilities.
- The author criticizes Gopinath for prioritizing negative market reactions over the intrinsic merits of these policies.
- It is noted that markets would also react negatively to inaction or failed fiscal consolidation, referencing the Eurozone's experience in the 2010s.

### Historical and Academic Precedents for Heterodox Policies
- The piece suggests that a sustained period of financial repression, with inflation at 3% or more, presents a viable policy option.
- Claudio Borio of the BIS reported in June 2019 that EMEs successfully combined inflation targeting with FX intervention and macroprudential measures, indicating a need for theoretical updates.
- A 2020 paper co-authored by Gopinath itself argued for moving beyond the traditional Mundell-Fleming framework.
- It proposed an integrated model to determine the optimal policy mix, acknowledging the complexities faced by these economies.

### Author's Conclusions and Commenter Perspectives
- The author concludes by expressing hesitancy regarding broad-based price controls.
- However, the author sees no viable alternative to financial repression, central bank-treasury coordination, and potentially targeted nationalization in the current environment.
- One commenter dissents, fearing that modifying central bank independence could serve right-wing interests, specifically mentioning the Kochs and Trumps.
- Another commenter adds that the "Four Horsemen" have been standard policy in EMEs like India since the 1970s.
- This commenter questions the transplantability of the political economy of financial repression to developed nations.
- They also note that these policies implicitly rely on a fifth assumption: bounded energy price variance, which current geopolitical events are actively dismantling.

## Conclusions and recommendations
- Implement central bank-treasury coordination.
- Consider targeted nationalization as a necessary measure.
