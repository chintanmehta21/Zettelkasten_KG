### Format and speakers

### Core argument
- A physics equation fundamentally altered financial risk management, leading to the creation of multi-trillion dollar derivatives markets. By applying scientific modeling to market dynamics, mathematicians and physicists have paradoxically pushed markets towards a state of greater efficiency where price movements become truly random.

## Chapter walkthrough

### The Challenge of Market Prediction
- Mathematician Jim Simons founded the Medallion investment fund, achieving an extraordinary 66% annual return for 30 years.
- This success contrasts with Isaac Newton's significant loss in the 1720 South Sea Company bubble, highlighting the difficulty of predicting market "madness.".
- Newton, despite his scientific genius, lost a third of his wealth after buying back into the South Sea Company at its peak.
- The concept of options, which are rights to buy or sell an asset, dates back to Thales of Miletus around 600 BC.
- Options provide limited downside risk, leverage, and hedging capabilities for investors.

### Louis Bachelier and Random Walks
- Louis Bachelier, a physics student in Paris, became fascinated by options while working at the Bourse in the late 19th century.
- For his PhD, advised by Henri Poincaré, Bachelier proposed that stock prices follow a random walk due to countless unpredictable factors.
- This random walk concept is a hallmark of an efficient market, where trading on predictions eliminates predictability.
- Bachelier used a Galton board analogy to show that while individual price movements are random, their collective possibilities form a spreading normal distribution.
- He independently rediscovered the heat equation, calling his version the "Radiation of Probabilities," a concept later developed by Einstein for Brownian motion.
- Bachelier's solution for option pricing aimed to equalize expected returns for both buyer and seller, but his work was largely ignored.

### Modern Option Pricing and Derivatives
- In the 1950s, physics PhD Ed Thorp applied his card-counting skills to the stock market, achieving 20% annual returns with dynamic hedging.
- Thorp developed an improved, but private, option pricing model in 1967 that included a "drift" for long-term trends.
- In 1973, Fischer Black and Myron Scholes published a superior option pricing model, with Robert Merton independently developing a similar version.
- The Black-Scholes-Merton model posits that a risk-free, delta-hedged portfolio of options and stocks should only earn the risk-free rate.
- This model provided an explicit mathematical formula for pricing derivatives, which was quickly adopted by Wall Street.
- The Chicago Board Options Exchange was founded in the same year, fueling explosive growth in the options market.

### Global Derivatives Market and Efficiency
- The Black-Scholes-Merton model facilitated the rapid expansion of the options market and other multi-trillion dollar markets like credit default swaps.
- Derivatives allow entities such as airlines to hedge against risks like fluctuating oil prices.
- They also provide significant leverage, as demonstrated by the GameStop short squeeze where Reddit users amplified buying power with options.
- The global derivatives market now totals several hundred trillion dollars, vastly exceeding the value of underlying securities.
- While derivatives enhance market liquidity in stable times, they can also intensify market crashes during periods of stress.
- The work of Merton and Scholes was recognized with a Nobel Prize in 1997, with Black acknowledged posthumously.
- Jim Simons' Renaissance Technologies hired scientists without finance backgrounds to find market patterns, challenging the efficient market hypothesis.

## Closing remarks
- Recap: The application of physics and mathematics to financial markets has not only created new industries but also, by systematically modeling and eliminating inefficiencies, has ironically pushed markets closer to a state of perfect efficiency. This continuous evolution means that while markets may become more predictable in some ways, they also become more random in others.
