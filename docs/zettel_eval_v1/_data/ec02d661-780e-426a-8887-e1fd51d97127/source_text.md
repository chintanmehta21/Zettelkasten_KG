## Software Stock Crash Driven by AI Displacement Fears
- A recent software stock crash, potentially irrational, is widely attributed to fears that AI is making software business models obsolete.
- Bloomberg reported hundreds of billions of dollars wiped from Silicon Valley company assets, and an iShares ETF tracking software stocks lost almost $1 trillion in value over seven days.
- This selloff, the largest driven by fear of AI displacement, was triggered by disappointing earnings, AI model improvements, and a new legal tool from AI startup Anthropic PBC.
- Software-as-a-service (SaaS) companies are most affected, with shares of Microsoft, Salesforce, Oracle, Intuit Inc., and AppLovin Corp. tumbling.
- Valuations are approaching the lows of the 2022 crash, and this is not part of a broader market downturn.

## AI Automation and the Obsolescence of Software Engineering Skills
- The author argues that human software engineers, likened to master craftsmen, are having their skills rendered obsolete by automation, similar to how the power loom displaced master weavers 200 years ago.
- AI coding tools enable relatively unskilled workers to produce software that is 'almost as good' and cheaper, a practice termed 'vibe coding' where software is 'conjured up rather than crafted.'
- Tools like Anthropic's Claude Code allow novices to create functional applications in hours by describing their needs in English, with the required technical knowledge expected to approach zero.
- Executives are discussing creating software businesses with zero developers.

### Expert Workflow Shifts
- Expert Andrej Karpathy stated his workflow shifted from 80% manual coding in November to 80% AI agent coding in December, calling it the biggest change to his workflow in ~20 years.
- Jeff Sandquist of Walmart Global Tech is quoted stating that much of engineering is 'drudgery,' suggesting software engineering is a 'routine cognitive' task vulnerable to automation.

### Future Role of Human Engineers
- While human expertise will still be needed to fix security flaws and tech debt in AI-generated code, the role will shift from a craft to factory-like maintenance.

## Profound Economic and Societal Shifts
- The author asserts that AI is making existing stocks of human capital obsolete.
- While it's uncertain if other fields like physics will follow, the current revolution will continue to upend technical experts' lives.
- This trend marks the end of an economic age defined by the rising wealth and status of technical professionals.
- It portends profound changes to careers, education, and economic organization.
