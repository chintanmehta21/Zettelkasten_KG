## Ethel Brooks' Experience and Amazon's Stance
- Ethel Brooks, an Amazon Fresh worker, contracted COVID-19 in March 2020 and was hospitalized for weeks at Cedars-Sinai.
- Brooks claims Amazon provided insufficient time off, gave her conflicting information, and dropped her from the schedule, leading to debt and eventual homelessness.
- A month-long email exchange with Amazon HR (HRC) in March-April 2020 showed Brooks seeking information on sick pay and job status, receiving unhelpful and conflicting responses from representatives.
- Brooks received two weeks of sick pay and an unexplained $200 check, which she stated was insufficient for her recovery.
- Amazon spokesperson Kelly Nantel stated The Markup provided limited time for investigation and that Brooks' case is not representative of the majority of employees.
- Nantel claimed Amazon's records show Brooks received responses, two weeks paid leave, approved unpaid time off, and later voluntarily resigned and was re-hired multiple times before being let go for not showing up to work.
- Brooks confirmed she later reapplied and left once she found another job.
- Brooks was hospitalized twice more with COVID and now works as a bus driver, but lives in a single-room occupancy hotel due to credit issues from incurred debt.

## Systemic Challenges and Evolving Policies
- Amazon reported no COVID-related worker hospitalizations or deaths to Cal/OSHA, despite 2020 news reports of multiple worker deaths in Southern California, Northern California, New York, Ohio, Indiana, and Illinois.
- Six other workers reported struggles to get adequate leave from Amazon, often dealing with automated HR processes (apps, chatbots).
- One former Georgia warehouse worker with asthma was told by HR to return to her station after an attack due to no accrued time off.
- Amazon has rolled back its COVID-19 sick leave policies: first shortening paid leave from two weeks to one in January, then eliminating paid leave entirely for U.S. workers, offering up to five days of unpaid, excused leave.
- Reports from The New York Times and NBC News indicate Amazon's automated systems have mistakenly terminated workers for absences, sent job-abandonment notices, and incorrectly deducted vacation time for sick leave.
- Other Amazon workers, including Natalie Monarrez in Staten Island and Vickie Shannon Allen in Texas, have also experienced homelessness.

## Reusable Ideas and Implications
- The critical impact of corporate HR policies and automated systems on worker well-being and financial stability, especially during public health crises.
- Potential discrepancies between corporate public statements/reported data and individual worker experiences.
- The vulnerability of hourly workers to financial precarity when sick leave and support systems are inadequate.
- The challenges of navigating complex, often automated, HR systems for essential information and support.
