## Overview
- This commentary discusses the "forward deployed engineer" as a pivotal new AI-era job.

### Format and speakers
- Format: Commentary.
- Speakers: Goldman Sachs.

### Core argument
- The speaker argues that the "forward deployed engineer" is the new pivotal job title in the AI era, representing a strategic move by AI companies to embed their talent within client organizations, which can lead to a dependency on the AI provider's services. This trend, originating from Palantir, is presented as a "con" that reshapes the enterprise software landscape and future job market.

## Chapter walkthrough

### The Rise of the Forward Deployed Engineer
- The speaker identifies "forward deployed engineer" as the new essential job title in the AI era.
- This term was originally coined by Palantir and represents a "humans as a service" model.
- Listeners are advised to update their LinkedIn titles to reflect this new role.
- Anthropic reportedly initiated a $1.5 billion joint venture with BlackRock and Goldman Sachs to deploy these engineers.
- OpenAI responded by launching a $14 billion venture named DeployCo, indicating a competitive landscape.
- This shift is attributed to AI's impact on enterprise engineers, reducing their motivation to engage in traditional coding.

### The "Slop Contractor" Model
- These "forward deployed engineers" are characterized as "slop contractors" hired by companies.
- Their primary purpose is to rapidly construct impressive AI demos and prototypes for clients.
- The resulting systems are often described as "slop" that functions well initially without significant user load.
- However, these systems typically fail or become unsustainable after approximately six months.
- By the time these issues arise, the contracted engineers have usually completed their engagement and departed.
- The speaker frames this entire process as a "con" where AI companies intentionally foster client team laziness.
- Subsequently, these AI companies then offer their own engineers as the necessary "antidote" to the problems they helped create.

### Future Scenarios for AI and Employment
- One presented future is a dystopian scenario within 10 years where companies cease hiring their own software engineers.
- Instead, they would exclusively contract engineers from dominant AI firms like Anthropic and OpenAI.
- These powerful AI companies would control access to advanced software-building models, such as hypothetical "Mythos" or "Opus 4.7.".
- Such firms could potentially grow into $100 trillion companies by gatekeeping these critical technologies.
- In this future, the general public would only have access to weaker AI models suitable for mundane tasks like cooking.
- A less alarming alternative scenario suggests that open-source AI models could advance sufficiently.
- This open-source progress would enable companies to continue developing "mid-grade software" internally.

### Job Market Advice for the AI Era
- For current job seekers, the speaker advises copying responsibilities directly from Anthropic's job postings for forward-deployed engineers.
- These responsibilities include building production applications using Claude, delivering technical artifacts, and maintaining expertise in LLMs.
- This strategy is claimed to ensure a callback from AI-powered resume screening systems.
- For interviews, candidates should study AI theory abstractly, emulating the rigorous preparation of "mini Dwarkesh" Patel.
- The speaker suggests using an AI tool like Claude during technical interviews to generate solutions for complex tasks.
- An example task given is building a multi-agent orchestration system, with the advice to then claim credit for the AI-generated output.

## Closing remarks
- Recap: The speaker concludes that adapting to the "forward deployed engineer" role, understanding its strategic implications, and leveraging AI tools for career advancement are crucial for navigating the evolving AI-driven job market. This approach, while potentially controversial, is presented as a pragmatic way to secure employment in the new tech landscape.
