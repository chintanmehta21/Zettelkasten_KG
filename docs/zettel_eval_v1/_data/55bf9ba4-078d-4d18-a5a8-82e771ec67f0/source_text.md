## Physiology of Breathing
- Breathing's primary function is to balance oxygen (O2) and carbon dioxide (CO2) ratios.
- Inhaled O2 is carried by hemoglobin to cells.
- CO2, while a waste product, is essential for changing hemoglobin's shape to release O2 into tissues.
- CO2 levels relate to body pH; during exercise, a slight pH drop increases O2 offloading to muscles.
- The brain, being highly metabolic, requires significant O2, and improper O2:CO2 ratios can cause hypoxia, impairing cognition.

## Common Breathing Patterns and Their Impact
- Healthy at-rest breathing is about 12 breaths per minute (~6 liters of air).
- Most people "overbreathe" at 15-18 breaths per minute, causing hypocapnia (low CO2).
- Hypocapnia reduces O2 delivery and can lead to anxiety and poor focus.
- To counter overbreathing, one can take small pauses between breaths and practice nasal breathing.

### Benefits of Nasal Breathing
- Increases resistance and extends exhales.
- Warms air.
- Produces nitric oxide, improving blood vessel dilation and relieving congestion.
- Claimed to improve facial aesthetics.

### Sleep Apnea (Under-breathing)
- Causes hypoxia and frequent waking at night.
- Symptoms include snoring and irritability.
- Risks include cardiovascular disease and stroke.
- Treatments include CPAP machines or mouth taping.

## Breathing Protocols for Health and Performance
- Breathing directly controls heart rate via respiratory sinus arrhythmia.
- Inhaling longer/more vigorously increases heart rate.
- Exhaling longer/more vigorously decreases heart rate.

### Carbon Dioxide Tolerance Test
- Developed by Brian Mackenzie.
- Measures the duration of a single, slow exhale after a full inhale to assess breathing efficiency.

### Box Breathing
- A pattern of inhale-hold-exhale-hold.
- Practicing for 2-3 minutes, 1-2 times per week, can improve diaphragmatic control and reduce overbreathing.

### Physiological Sigh (Stress Reduction)
- Described as the fastest way to calm the autonomic nervous system.
- Involves two consecutive nasal inhales followed by a long mouth exhale.
- A clinical trial showed 5 minutes of daily practice reduces stress, improves sleep, and lowers resting heart rate.

### Cyclic Hyperventilation (Alertness)
- Involves 25 cycles of deep nasal inhales and mouth exhales, followed by a 15-30 second breath-hold.
- Releases adrenaline to enhance focus.
- Caution is advised for those with anxiety, and it must never be performed near water or while driving due to the risk of passing out.
