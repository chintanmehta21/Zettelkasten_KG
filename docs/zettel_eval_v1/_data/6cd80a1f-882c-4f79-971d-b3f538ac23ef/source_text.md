## Definition and Core Concept
- A Zettelkasten (German for 'slipbox') is a personal knowledge management system.
- It uses information on slips or cards (Zetteln) linked by metadata (subject headings, numbers, or tags).
- Primarily used for research, study, and writing.

## Historical Origins and Early Development
- Originated from the commonplace book tradition.

### Key Figures and Contributions
- Conrad Gessner (1516–1565): Proposed rearranging notes by gluing slips onto sheets.
- Thomas Harrison (c. 1640s): Designed a card cabinet called the 'ark of studies' (Arca studiorum).
- Vincent Placcius (1689): Improved the system in his book *De arte excerpendi*.
- Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716): Utilized the system.
- Carl Linnaeus (1767): Used over 1,000 five-by-three-inch paper slips for his research.
- Johann Jacob Moser (1701–1785): Described algorithms for his card boxes.
- Jean Paul (1796): Structured his idyll *Leben des Quintus Fixlein* based on a Zettelkasten; assembled 12,000 paper scraps.
- Charles-Victor Langlois and Charles Seignobos (1897): Recommended the system for historians.

### Early Adoption Context
- Still considered a novelty in America in the 1890s.

## 20th Century Evolution and Manuals
- Antonin Sertillanges' *The Intellectual Life* (1921) detailed a card-file method, referencing Paul Chavigny's 1918 decimal system.
- Numerous English-language research manuals published from the 1920s to the 1980s provided instructions.

## Prominent Users and Their Systems

### Academics and Theorists
- Frederic L. Paxson (historian): Used 80 drawers of 3x5-inch slips.
- Walter Benjamin (philosopher): Used a numbered system for his *Arcades Project* (1927-1940).
- Roland Barthes (theorist): Bequest includes 12,250 slips; used for works like *Michelet* and *Mourning Diary*.
- Hans Blumenberg (philosopher): Maintained over 30,000 cards.
- Niklas Luhmann (sociologist): Credited his 90,000-card system (digitized in 2019) for his prolific output (about 50 books and 550 articles).
- Mario Bunge (philosopher-scientist): Produced c. 70 books and 540 articles.

### Writers
- Arno Schmidt (German writer): Over 100,000 cards for *Zettels Traum*.
- Walter Kempowski (German writer).

## Modern Influence and Cultural Footprint
- In the 1980s, the card file became a metaphor for hypertext software like Xerox PARC's NoteCards.
- Inspired the invention of wikis in the 1990s.
- Referenced in literary works by Jean Paul (1796), Anatole France (1908), Robert M. Pirsig (1991).
- Analyzed in Markus Krajewski's *Paper Machines* (2002).
