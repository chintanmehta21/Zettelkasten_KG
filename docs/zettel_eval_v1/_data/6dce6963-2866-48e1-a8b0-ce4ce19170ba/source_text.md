## Investigation Findings
- Bellingcat and Colombian media outlet Cerosetenta linked Colombian politician and businessman Jorge Rodriguez to the neo-Nazi group Active Club Bogota.
- A video from February 26 shows Rodriguez and other men painting over graffiti in Bogota's Restrepo neighborhood.
- Rodriguez claimed on Instagram they were 'defending public space to stop generating hatred,' but they covered a message reading, 'Creole Nazis will not pass.'

## Evidence Linking Rodriguez to Active Club Bogota
- A pixelated man in the video has tattoos with multiple similarities to Javier “Orlik” Ruiz, a prominent member of Active Club Bogota.
- Bellingcat identified Ruiz by matching tattoos on his hands, fingers (including an arrow on his left middle finger), and arms from unpixelated frames with numerous public photos.
- Rodriguez follows Ruiz on Instagram.
- Ruiz 'liked' the video posted by Rodriguez.
- Active Club Bogota's official Instagram account is one of just over 60 accounts that follows Rodriguez.
- In March, Rodriguez also 'liked' a March 2026 post from the group featuring a neo-Nazi movement flag.

## Active Club Bogota Profile and Activities
- Active Club Bogota has been online since early 2024.
- It is the only official South American chapter of the international network started by US white supremacist Robert Rundo.
- The network uses fitness and fighting to recruit young men.
- The Bogota chapter hosted Adolf Hitler birthday celebrations at a community centre in 2025 (with a book burning) and 2026 (with swastika cupcakes and a 1940 Nazi propaganda film).

## Political Context and Reactions
- Rodriguez unsuccessfully ran for congress in the March 2026 elections with the Centro Democratico party, receiving 4,401 votes.
- He presents himself as a prominent Centro Democratico member and has appeared with party figures including Rafael Nieto Loaiza, Gabriel Vallejo, Paloma Valencia, and Alvaro Uribe Velez.
- Centro Democratico's National Director stated the party was unaware of any proven links and does not endorse groups that incite hate.
- Experts Alexander Ritzmann (CEP) and Heidi Beirich (GPAHE) warned that such affiliations lend legitimacy to neo-Nazi groups and that even small chapters can be recruitment hubs for transnational networks.

## Legal Responses and Journalistic Justification
- Both Rodriguez and Ruiz responded to Bellingcat's queries with legal threats, citing Colombian data protection laws, but did not answer questions.
- Bellingcat and Cerosetenta consulted legal experts and determined their reporting is protected by freedom of expression.
- They cited public interest and the subjects' status as 'public figures' as justification for their reporting.

## International Neo-Nazi Network Links
- The investigation suggests Active Club Bogota has links to international neo-Nazi networks Blood & Honour and Combat 18.
- There is no evidence connecting Rodriguez directly to Blood & Honour or Combat 18.
- Evidence includes a May 2024 photo of a man with Ruiz's tattoos next to Blood & Honour musician Ken McLellan in Bogota.
- Another associate sent gifts (including an Active Club Bogota sticker) to the alleged leader of Combat 18 in Spain.
- A neo-Nazi fashion retailer listing Ruiz as a contact was banned from WhatsApp by Meta after Bellingcat reached out.
- Combat 18 is designated a terrorist organization in Canada and is subject to UK financial sanctions.
