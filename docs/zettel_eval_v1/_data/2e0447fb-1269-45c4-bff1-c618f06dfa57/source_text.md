## Historical Parallel: Japan's Economic Trajectory
- Compares current 'Chinese Century' conventional wisdom to 1980s predictions of Japanese dominance.
- Ezra Vogel's 'Japan As Number One' predicted Japanese supremacy.
- Bill Emmott's 1989 book 'The Sun Also Sets' more accurately predicted Japan's reversion to the mean.
- Emmott identified Japan's financial weakness, aging population, and low service-sector productivity as key vulnerabilities.

## Prevailing Views on China's Rise
- Western skepticism of China has largely evaporated.
- Attention is focused on Chinese cars, cities, and trade surpluses.
- Perceived U.S. dysfunction is often contrasted with China's progress.
- Most Americans believe China has or will soon overtake the U.S.

## Author's Prior Assessment of China
- Previously assessed that the 21st century would be a Chinese one, albeit with less pronounced dominance than America's in the 20th century.
- Argued China's size (12 times Japan's) allows for industrial and geopolitical dominance without high per capita GDP.
- Dismissed demographics and housing as major threats to China's rise.

## Recent Shift in Author's Perspective
- In the last couple of months, the author has grown more skeptical.
- Believes China may be stumbling in ways that will become apparent in a decade or two.
- This shift is based on four factors.

### Limits of Industrial Policy
- China's industrial policy is hitting its limits faster than expected.

### AI and Technological Advantage
- The rise of AI agents makes China's technological advantage less defensible.

### Leadership Dynamics
- Xi Jinping is entering a paranoid 'Death of Stalin' phase earlier than anticipated.

### Potential Military Weakness
- Former President Trump's attacks on Venezuela and Iran suggest potential Chinese military weakness.
